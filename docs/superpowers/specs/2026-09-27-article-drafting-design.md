# Article Drafting: Templates, Style Rules and Images

Status: draft for review. Applies to Phase 4 and later (research and drafting). Nothing here is built yet.

## Goal

Define how article drafts are shaped, so output is consistent, reviewable in about 10 minutes, and never signals AI authorship to readers.

## Decisions

1. **Fixed templates, chosen by rule, not at random.** Random structure makes drafts hard to review and hard to calibrate during the trial. Variety comes from having a few distinct shapes.
2. **One constant voice.** Templates change structure, not voice. Voice rules live in one style guide used by every template.
3. **Template choice is a pure function** of the topic card's signal type and the research package. The editor can override it at draft review.

## Templates (V1)

| Template | Used for | Target length | Skeleton |
|---|---|---|---|
| News brief | Release, launch, announcement | 250 to 400 words | Headline, one-paragraph lede (what happened, who, when), "What changed" bullets, "Why it matters" paragraph, sources |
| Explainer | Concept, technique, trend | 700 to 1000 words | Headline, lede, "The short version", 2 to 4 titled sections, "What to watch", sources |
| Paper breakdown | Research paper | 500 to 800 words | Headline, lede, "The problem", "The approach", "What the results show", "Limits", sources |
| Comparison | Model or tool versus peers | 600 to 900 words | Headline, lede, comparison table (claims with sources), "Where each fits", sources |

Selection rule (first match wins): source type `paper` gives Paper breakdown; card tags include a comparison signal gives Comparison; a release or launch signal gives News brief; otherwise Explainer.

Every template outputs the same structured shape: `title`, `dek`, `sections[]`, `claims[]` (each mapped to evidence), `sources[]`, `cover` (see Images). The claim map is what the review summary and verification stage read.

## Style rules (apply to every template)

- No em dashes and no en dashes used as dashes. Use commas, colons, periods or parentheses.
- No first-person or experiential claims ("I tested", "we ran") unless an executed experiment in the research package supports them (PRD decision 10).
- No mention of AI authorship, models used, or generation, anywhere in reader-facing text or metadata.
- Every factual claim maps to evidence in the research package. Unsupported claims are cut, not softened.
- Plain, specific language. No hype words the ranker already penalises.
- Length targets above are limits the writer is told about and the publish gate checks.

Enforcement: the writer prompt states the rules; a deterministic publish-gate check rejects a draft that contains an em dash, an en dash used as a dash, or banned authorship phrases. The gate fails closed, and the editor sees the reason.

## Images

- Every article has a `cover` slot: `{ src?: string, alt: string, credit?: string }`. `alt` is required.
- No `src`: the reader site renders a deterministic token-styled fallback (`CoverImage` in the design system), seeded from the slug. No empty or broken boxes.
- V1 image sources: none generated by AI. Allowed later: the source's own Open Graph image where its terms permit, or an editor upload. Credit is shown when the source requires it.
- Open Graph image for sharing: V1 uses a generated text card from the title and tokens, not a model-generated image.

## Editor-only data

Model trail, prompt version, template choice, verification notes and the risk summary are visible under `/editor` only. Public routes never receive these fields (the public API shape omits them, and a web test scans public sources).

## Out of scope

Image generation, per-topic custom templates, A/B testing of structure, translations.

## Open questions

- Word-count targets are starting points; calibrate on the first 2 weeks of drafts.
- Whether "Comparison" needs its own evidence rules (benchmark claims are high risk).
