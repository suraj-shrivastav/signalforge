Sparse attention cuts long-context cost by a third

A new attention pattern keeps quality on 128k-token inputs while reducing compute. The gains are real, but they shrink on short prompts.

SignalForge Desk27 Sep 2026 6 min read

Long inputs are expensive because attention cost grows with the square of the sequence. The paper replaces full attention in most layers with a fixed sparse pattern and keeps a few dense layers for global context.

What the results show

  • About 33% lower compute at 128k tokens on the authors' benchmark set.
  • Quality within 1 point of the dense baseline on retrieval tasks.
  • No gain below 8k tokens, where dense attention is already cheap.
The saving depends on keeping the dense layers. Removing them cost 6 points.

Code and weights are not released yet, so the numbers have no independent check.
